01 Fundamentals

Teduc in 30 seconds

Institutional
In short
  • Teduc is an ecosystem connecting education, AI, product, technology, content, research, innovation and business.
  • Present that breadth from the other person's need, never as a catalog.
  • Network figures and client names are evidence of capability, not a promise of results.

We develop people, transform organizations and build solutions, products and assets. The client does not need to understand the whole ecosystem; they need to recognize that Teduc knows how to solve their problem.

Adjust the emphasis to the person in front of you. A company struggling to implement AI wants to see how Teduc helps choose applications and put them to use. An education group with a content demand wants to see educational, editorial and operational capability.

We start from the outcome that needs to be reached and bring together the capabilities required to build, implement and evaluate what makes sense in that context.”

What backs our credibility

The network brings together more than 3,000 professionals, 75% of whom hold a master's, a doctorate or a specialist degree, present in more than 320 cities and 20 states (Teduc internal data, as of September 2026). These numbers describe the network, not a fixed team available for any project.

Instituto Ayrton Senna, Pearson, Cogna Educação, Grupo SEB and Conexia are part of Teduc's track record. Cite them exactly that way: “organizations that are part of our track record.” Contract type, scope, results and disclosure permission must be confirmed before any detail is shared.

Avoid

An illustrative example

An education group asks for “AI training for the whole team.” Investigation shows the priority challenge is reducing rework in producing materials without losing quality. Training becomes part of the answer, alongside redesigning the workflow, usage guidelines and a test on a bounded set of materials.

The sentence that organizes any opportunity

Template · problem statement

When to use it. In discovery, in the Opportunity & Fit session, when reviewing the proposal and when evaluating the project.

03 Fundamentals

The Business Partner's role

Recommended practiceSet by agreement
In short
  • You connect problems, relationships and capabilities. The exact role is agreed on each opportunity.
  • Scope, price, deadline, team and performance are only committed with Teduc's validation.
  • Good work leaves the client better oriented and Teduc better informed.

A Business Partner can identify a demand, co-create an offer, lead the commercial relationship, contribute to delivery or take an asset to new markets.

Core responsibilities

  • Understand and record precisely. Separate facts, hypotheses and open questions.
  • Present Teduc with current materials and point to the front with the closest fit to the need.
  • Bring Teduc in before committing to scope, price, deadline, team or performance.
  • Keep the history current and respect relationships that already exist with the account.
  • Ensure a clean handoff to the delivery team and stay involved as agreed.

How to introduce yourself

Script · introducing yourself

“I'm a Teduc business partner, and I work connecting organizations' needs to the ecosystem's capabilities. My role in this conversation is to understand your challenge and, with the Teduc team, identify the best way forward.”

Careful. Do not introduce yourself as an employee, shareholder or representative with signing authority unless that is formally established. The title “Business Partner,” on its own, does not create those relationships.

Quality bar. Every conversation should produce understanding and a possible decision, even when the best decision is not to move forward right now.

How to choose the model

  1. List what each side actually brings: relationships, expertise, technology, distribution, data, product, capital or execution.
  2. Identify the first deliverable and the level of integration it requires.
  3. Start with the simplest model that solves it. Integration can grow with the evidence.

Example. A consultant identifies a demand and Teduc handles the entire delivery: that is Teduc delivery, with a commission to be agreed. If that consultant also facilitates workshops within the project, that work requires its own scope, acceptance and fees, on top of the commercial participation.

Worth noting. Joint development does not mean an automatic partnership. Sharing an opportunity does not, by itself, define ownership of the client, the product or future revenue.

Three entry formats

When there is doubt

Diagnostic

There is a relevant need but no priorities, requirements or direction. The deliverable must guide a decision, not just describe problems.

When there is a hypothesis

Pilot or lab

The hypothesis is clear enough for a bounded test. It needs an audience, duration, scope, evaluation criteria and a follow-up decision.

When there is clarity

Project or program

Outcome and implementation conditions are understood. It needs governance, deliverables and acceptance criteria.

How to expand without losing focus

Faced with an adjacent need, ask: is it a dependency of the current outcome or a future opportunity? If it is a dependency, discuss including it in scope. If it can wait, record it for a later review.

Education and content as a capability

Editorial and educational demands can be qualified here, with scope confirmed by Teduc: audiences, materials, stages, volume, specialties and quality criteria. Ask for authorized samples, specifications, quantity, calendar and approval flow. Authoring, review, design and technology carry different effort: do not price by page count.

Questions that open the conversation

  • Which process, result or experience needs to improve?
  • What has already been tried, and what blocked implementation?
  • Who will use the solution, and who will be responsible for sustaining it?
  • Which systems, data, materials and specialists need to take part?
  • What does the client need to see in order to approve the next step?

How to build a viable entry point

Problem still open: propose a diagnostic. Enough clarity: discuss a bounded implementation. Recurring need: look into a program or continuous operation, subject to approved capacity and commercial model.

Limit of promise. A capability existing in the ecosystem does not mean it is immediately available, in any combination.

How to qualify a demand

Look into audience, prior experience, estimated number of participants, objective, availability, format and ability to mobilize people. In education, who buys, who attends and who evaluates are usually different people; identify all three.

Ask which deliverables would demonstrate learning and application: better-grounded decisions for leaders, a usable prototype in a lab, guided execution of a process in team training.

The boundary with Teduc Business

A company that wants to develop its team may enter through Business and bring in Education. The client should not have to coordinate two disconnected proposals: name one owner for the opportunity and present an integrated offer.

Before promoting anything. Confirm the current catalog, cohorts, dates, prices, faculty, requirements, the nature of the certification and the responsible institution. The names of the fronts in the deck do not prove that every program is open for enrollment.

How to handle a product possibility

Identify the user, the recurring problem, the current alternative, the willingness to use or buy, and the channel of access. Assess what can be reused across clients and what depends on customization. A technically interesting solution may have no demand or viable economics.

Confirm with the asset owner what is live, in testing or planned. A demo must match the version and scope available.

Before co-creating an asset

Agree on the problem, market hypothesis, contributions, owners, investment ceiling and continuity criteria. Define how pre-existing materials, new development, data, brand, commercialization, support and termination will be treated.

Client data. The possibility of reuse does not authorize taking a client's content, data or confidential materials.

Example. A partner has access to a sector with a relevant repetitive task. The first step can test usefulness and adoption with authorized users. Only after assessing the evidence does it make sense to discuss wider distribution, licensing or a new venture.

Prepare each account before reaching out

When to pause or close

Pause when

  • The demand has no owner.
  • An essential condition is missing.
  • The client cannot prioritize the conversation.

Close when

  • There is a clear mismatch of scope.
  • Infeasibility is confirmed.
  • An inappropriate requirement appears.

Partnership criterion. Beyond revenue, look for distribution, capability, technology, intellectual property, market or proof. A strong partnership creates at least two of those assets.

13 Opportunity

Outreach and first conversation

Recommended practice
In short
  • One true observation, one explicit hypothesis and one low-effort invitation.
  • Never assign a problem to the client without confirmation.
  • Always end with an agreed action, not with “let me know if you need anything.”
Message · known contact

“Hi [name]. I work as a business partner for Teduc, which connects education, AI, product and technology. Seeing [real context], I thought it would make sense to talk about [challenge or opportunity]. Is that among your priorities? If so, we could set aside twenty minutes to understand the situation and assess a fit.”

Message · referral

“Hi [name]. [Person] suggested this conversation and allowed me to mention the referral. I work with Teduc and I'd like to understand how you are handling [topic]. The idea is to assess whether our capabilities can contribute to [plausible outcome], starting from your context.”

When they ask for “a presentation” right away

Send the current institutional material with two lines about the connection that prompted your outreach, and propose a short conversation to identify the relevant front. Sending the deck on its own does not qualify the opportunity.

Script · opening the meeting

“I'd like to understand what you need to solve, what already exists and what result would make a difference. Then I'll share where Teduc can contribute and we'll agree whether there's a useful next step. Does that work?”

Closing the first conversation

Recap the problem in the client's own words, confirm your reading and agree on an action: a meeting with someone else, sending information, a session with Teduc or a deliberate pause.

Message · follow-up

“In our conversation, [priority] came up as the central point. To assess [next step], we still need to confirm [information]. Does it make sense to pick this up on [date], or has the priority changed?”

Cadence. Agree the next date during the conversation itself. With no reply, send one contextual follow-up and then a respectful closing message. Persistence with no new information wears the relationship down.

Discovery meeting

Recommended practice
In short
  • 45 minutes as a reference, in four blocks. The script is support, not an interrogation.
  • Ask for concrete situations before using words like transformation or productivity.
  • Minimum output: problem, outcome, people, conditions, open questions, priority and next step.
TimeBlockGuiding questions
5 minContext“How does this work today?” “Who is affected?”
20 minProblem and impact“Where does the work get stuck?” “Can you give a recent example?” “What has been tried?” “What happens when the problem repeats?” “Do you track time, cost, rework, quality or adoption?”

Script · closing

“So [problem] causes [consequence], and you want [outcome]. We still need to confirm [gaps]. I propose [action], with [people], by [date]. Is that reading correct?”

When to bring in specialists

Call on Teduc when integrations, technical requirements, sensitive data, academic criteria, large scale, contractual restrictions or questions requiring specialist assessment come up. Record the question instead of improvising an answer.

What does not have to happen here. You do not need to design the solution or talk price. Discovery should also not turn, without agreement, into an extensive technical diagnostic or free consulting. Consolidate everything in the opportunity brief.

16 Opportunity

Registering and attributing opportunities

Recommended practiceSet by agreement
In short
  • Check, register, get confirmation and only then act on behalf of the partnership.
  • Registering first does not guarantee commission or exclusivity.
  • Attribution conflicts are settled with Teduc, never in front of the client.

Flow for a new opportunity

  1. Check with your contact whether the account or demand is already being served.
  2. Register the information in the system Teduc has defined.
  3. Get confirmation of the Teduc owner and of your participation.
  4. Act: organize joint contacts and activities on behalf of the partnership.

Minimum data in the record

  • Identifier, organization, unit and opportunity name.
  • Partner who registered it, source of the contact, date and relevant history.
  • Contact person, professional role and authorized channel.
  • Problem, expected outcome and entry vertical.
  • Current stage, estimated value with its assumptions and decision horizon.
  • Teduc owner, next step, date and open items.
  • Partnership model and status of the commercial validation.

While there is no official tool, propose a simple base with restricted access and the same fields for every partner.

Existing accounts and multiple origins

Describe what is genuinely new and what you bring; Teduc assesses overlap, continuity and participation. If two partners present the same demand, preserve dates, records and contributions and take the case to the Teduc owner. Do not start competing conversations while the alignment is pending.

Update routine

Update after every relevant interaction. Separate what the client confirmed from what you estimate. If the date of a next action passes, reschedule it, record the reason or move the opportunity out of the active stage. When reactivating an old opportunity, review scope, contacts and terms: history does not define rights over new revenue.

To be formalized by Teduc. Registration validity, commercial protection, territory, splits between partners and the treatment of existing accounts. This handbook does not set those parameters.

18 Proposal

Building a consultative proposal

Recommended practice
In short
  • A good proposal answers: why act, what will be delivered, how, what it depends on and what it costs.
  • Teduc validates the solution and closes the sale.
  • Sending the document is a step, not the end of the negotiation.

Seven blocks

  1. Context and problem, using the facts the client provided.
  2. Expected outcome and the criteria that will guide the assessment.
  3. Scope, deliverables and limits of each stage.
  4. Method, governance and responsibilities.
  5. Schedule, milestones and client dependencies.
  6. Investment, terms, validity and commercial assumptions.
  7. Acceptance, formalization and next step.

Scope that can be executed

“AI program” is not scope. Define audience, number of sessions or activities, materials, support, format and expected evidence. In technology, bound the functions, environments, integrations, data, acceptance testing and support. Record the relevant exclusions: the client needs to know what they will have to contract or provide.

Acceptance criteria

For each deliverable, state who evaluates it, against which requirements and through which process. Separate conformance of the deliverable (within the team's control) from business results (which also depend on adoption, data and client decisions).

Commercial alternatives

Where there are real choices, present an essential entry option and an extended one, explaining the difference in outcome, scope and investment. Do not create artificial options to anchor price.

Before sending

  • Feasibility confirmed with the technical team.
  • Capacity and schedule confirmed with operations.
  • Terms confirmed with the commercial owner.
  • Contract review where needed.
  • Approved version identified, and who may present it.

When presenting

  • Discuss it in a meeting whenever possible.
  • Go back to the problem before talking scope.
  • Explain the choices you made.
  • Confirm the decision path.

Review the proposal with the proposal checklist before the final send.

19 Proposal

Pricing and negotiation

Recommended practiceSet by agreement
In short
  • Price matches scope, complexity, responsibility and execution conditions.
  • You help surface value and constraints; Teduc validates the investment and authorizes terms.
  • Before discussing a discount, find out what is behind the request.

What improves an estimate

Volume, profiles involved, degree of customization, formats, integrations, licenses, travel, revisions, support, timeline and dependencies. An estimate with explicit assumptions is worth more than a precise number built on an unknown scope. Indicative ranges only with authorized figures, and always with the conditions that can change them.

Script · investment

“To size a viable solution, we need to understand the priority and the investment conditions. Is there a range in mind, or an internal process for building that budget?”

If the client cannot answer, explore staged alternatives and how similar investments get approved. Do not assume there is no budget, nor unlimited ability to pay.

Client request and how to assess it

RequestPath
Lower investmentRevisit priorities and propose a smaller first stage.
Shorter timelineCheck capacity, dependencies and the impact on effort.
More deliverables for the same priceIdentify substitutions or request a scope review.
Different payment termsSubmit it to commercial and financial review.
Guaranteed resultsSeparate a delivery commitment from results that depend on third parties.

Record the negotiation

Keep a list of requested adjustments, their impact, who approved them and the resulting version. Forgotten verbal concessions are a frequent source of misalignment.

Essential conduct. Do not fund a discount out of your commission, and do not offer third-party work without explicit agreement between the parties.

20 Proposal

Answering questions and objections

Recommended practice
In short
  • Listen until you understand what the objection means.
  • Acknowledge it, investigate the cause and answer with a fact, an alternative or a verifiable next step.
  • Do not use rehearsed lines to dodge a legitimate question.
“We already have a team or a supplier.”
Answer

“It makes sense to keep what already works. Where do you still see a gap in capability, expertise or implementation?”

With no relevant gap, do not force an overlap. With a gap, assess a complementary contribution with clear roles.

“It's expensive.”
Answer

“Which part of the investment was furthest from your expectation? Are you comparing the same scope, or do you need a smaller entry point?”

Go back to priorities and consider a scope adjustment. A discount alone does not fix a proposal that was misunderstood.

“We just need a course.”
Answer

“We can look at that. What do participants need to do differently after the training?”

If a course meets the objective, keep it simple. If there are barriers to application, explain why another capability may be needed.

“Can you guarantee the productivity gain?”
Answer

“We can define how to measure it, and commitments about the work we contract. The gain depends on the process, the conditions and adoption. Let's establish the baseline and assess a first intervention before projecting scale.”

“I want to see the technology working.”
Answer

“I'll arrange with Teduc a demo of what is available for this context. We also need to confirm which requirements of your environment it has to meet.”

Distinguish a demo, a prototype and a production-ready solution.

“It's not a priority yet.”
Answer

“What would have to change for this to reach the agenda? Is there a date or decision that would justify picking it up again?”

Record the trigger and pause the opportunity.

“Why work with Teduc?”
Answer

“Teduc brings together education, AI, product, technology and a network of specialists. For your challenge, the contribution is combining [relevant capabilities] into a delivery with [outcome and how it is assessed].”

Use track-record proof that is relevant and authorized.

“Could we do a free pilot?”
Answer

“We need to assess the effort and the objective. A test requires scope, resources and decision criteria. I'll check with Teduc what format is viable and under what conditions.”

Do not assume it is free, nor that pre-sales work is unlimited.

21 Proposal

Closing and handing over to delivery

Recommended practice
In short
  • Expressed interest is not a formalized contract.
  • The handoff passes on context, commitments and sensitive points, including what was discussed and not contracted.
  • Scope changes always go through review and formalization.

Checks before kickoff

Handoff meeting

You and the commercial owner present the delivery team with the context, the motivation behind the purchase, the expectations, the approved commitments and the sensitive points. Also record what was discussed and not contracted, so an exploratory idea does not resurface as an obligation.

Kickoff with the client

Align objective, people, stages, channels, follow-up schedule, dependencies and the approval process. Confirm who can request changes and who can authorize them. The client should not receive different guidance from you and from the Teduc team.

Scope change

Describe the change, the reason and the impact on effort, timeline, cost and outcome. Send it for review and formalization before taking on the work. You can help clarify the need, but you do not authorize extra work.

The handoff template goes with the final proposal. Its job is to preserve context and responsibility when the people who sell and the people who execute are different.

22 Value

Follow-up and evidence of value

Recommended practice
In short
  • Three different levels: delivered, in use, changed.
  • Few indicators, tied to the original problem and possible to track.
  • With no baseline, there is no way to claim a gain.
Level 1 · Delivery

Was it done and accepted?

Material approved, training delivered, feature accepted.

Level 2 · Use

Have people adopted it?

Access, tasks performed, applied participation, recurring use.

Level 3 · Result

Did the original problem change?

Time, quality, rework or another agreed indicator.

How to define the assessment

For each indicator, record the definition, the baseline, the data source, the frequency and the owner. Targets are agreed from the context, not copied from another project. With no baseline, agree how to observe it before or at the start of the intervention. If the data cannot be obtained, describe the limitation and use complementary evidence.

Script · value review

“What has been delivered? What is in use? What still blocks application? What signs of improvement are there? What changed in the context? What decision do we need to make now?”

The delivery team leads the follow-up. You take part as agreed, help listen to the client and spot needs worth a later commercial assessment.

When something goes off track

Record the fact, its impact and the response needed. Escalate to the right owner and agree on a reply. Do not assign blame, announce compensation or promise a fix before assessing it with Teduc.

Case studies and testimonials. They require content validation and permission. Never use private testimonials, client figures or pilot results in commercial material without clearance.

Conduct, data and frequently asked questions

Recommended practiceSet by agreement
In short
  • Brand, case studies and co-branding are always aligned with Teduc.
  • Client data: only what is needed, through the right channels, with confirmed permission, including in AI tools.
  • When in doubt about rights, what counts is what your agreement says.

Brand and materials

Use approved presentations and descriptions. Preserve the logo's proportions and versions from the official files; ask for the right asset instead of redrawing it. Publications, events, ads, co-branded offers and the use of case studies go through alignment with Teduc.

Client information and use of AI

Share only what is necessary, with the right people and through the right channels. Confirm permissions before putting client documents into external tools, including AI tools. Do not use third-party credentials and do not promise security, retention or data-handling conditions without technical and contractual validation.

If something is sent in error or exposure is suspected: stop sharing, notify your Teduc contact and preserve the information needed for review.

Frequently asked questions

Can I work across more than one vertical?
Yes. The model allows capabilities to be combined; fit and availability are assessed by Teduc. Keep one owner for the opportunity and one coherent solution for the client.
Can I promise exclusivity?
Only with a formal agreement setting out reach, term and conditions. The institutional proposal does not establish exclusivity or any automatic reservation of territory or segment.
Does registering an opportunity guarantee a commission?
Not on its own. Recognition of participation and compensation are confirmed in the applicable agreement. See chapters 05 and 16.
Can I negotiate directly with the client?
In a co-created offer, you may lead the sale, which Teduc then closes. Negotiation limits, discounts and commitments must be aligned; signing requires specific authorization.
Can I refer and also execute?
Yes, joint delivery is provided for. Separate commercial participation, execution scope and the corresponding fees, and confirm capacity before offering your work.
Can I promote products or programs from the deck?
Confirm current availability, terms and messaging. The deck presents assets and fronts, but does not detail every feature, cohort or purchasable offer.
How does ending the partnership work?
It depends on the agreement signed. Align open opportunities, transfer of contacts, access, materials and pending obligations. Do not assume commission or brand use continues afterwards.
